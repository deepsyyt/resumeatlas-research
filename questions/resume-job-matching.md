# Cluster: resume–job matching

**The core confusion.** How close a match is close enough — and why an apparently
qualified candidate still gets rejected.

## Recurring themes

- Whether to apply without meeting every requirement
- What proportion of requirements must be met
- What separates a "required" qualification from a "preferred" one, and how
  seriously to take the distinction
- Being rejected despite appearing to match the posting
- Whether to tailor a resume for each application, or whether that is wasted effort
- How to gain experience when postings all ask for experience

## What we can answer

Three findings bear directly on this cluster:

- Median keyword coverage against the target posting is high, so by the time
  someone runs a check, the words are largely already present —
  [alignment](../research/resume-job-alignment.md)
- Most gaps are framed **required**, not preferred, so the required/preferred
  distinction matters less than the advice implies —
  [skill gaps](../research/skill-gap-analysis.md)
- Median resume experience and median posting requirement sit close together, so
  the experience gap is not the main barrier in this sample

Taken together these suggest the "rejected despite matching" theme has a
structural explanation: resumes in this corpus are keyword-complete and
evidence-poor. See [keywords are not the bottleneck](../insights/keywords-are-not-the-bottleneck.md).

## What we cannot answer

Whether tailoring changes an outcome. We can measure what a tailored resume
changes *in the document*; we cannot observe what happened to the application.
